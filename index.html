Insights & recommended actions

What the numbers say, and what to decide next.
Act now

May's margin squeeze came from COGS, not overheads

COGS climbed to 76.5% of revenue in May from 72.0% in April. That 4.6-point shift alone cost ≈ ₹5.0 L of gross profit — the bulk of the ₹5.8 L fall in monthly PAT.

Action: audit May's input costs, pricing and product mix. Simply restoring April's COGS ratio adds back ~₹5 L of profit per month.

Review

May's "marketing spike" is mostly discounts, not ads

Of May's ₹5.38 L marketing, ₹2.30 L is "Discount allowed" — price concessions booked as marketing (April: ₹0.99 L). Listing fees were flat at ₹1.5 L both months. Together with the COGS ratio jump, May's real problem is pricing and discounting, not ad spend.

Action: track discounts as a revenue deduction, set a per-channel discount policy, and review May's big discount recipients.

Watch

The break-even cushion nearly vanished in May

At May's 23.5% gross margin, ~₹99 L of monthly revenue is needed to cover the ₹23.2 L of non-COGS costs — May's ₹1.08 Cr cleared it by a whisker. At April's 28.0% margin, break-even was only ~₹74 L.

Action: set a floor gross margin (e.g. 26%+) as the operating target; below it, profit disappears at current overhead levels.

On track

Scale-up has fixed the fixed-cost problem

Revenue run-rate is ~₹1.05 Cr/month vs ₹44.8 L/month averaged across FY 2025-26 (2.3×). Employee cost is down from 10.1% of revenue last year to 8.5% now — overheads are being absorbed.

Action: protect the volume gains; they are what turned last year's loss into this year's profit.

On track

Overheads are well behaved

Employee benefits (+7.1%) and transportation (+7.9%) tracked revenue growth. Finance cost fell 16.3% and other expenses fell 7.5% — no cost-control issue outside COGS and marketing.

Note

Monthly profits are before depreciation & tax

April/May statements book no depreciation; FY 2025-26 carried ₹5.69 L (~₹47 K/month). True monthly PAT is therefore slightly lower than shown — May's thin ₹2.18 L cushion is thinner still.

Action: accrue depreciation monthly so month-end profit reflects reality.

Act now

Profits are turning into receivables, not cash

PAT was +₹10.1 L over April–May, but debtors grew ₹44.9 L (₹32.2 L → ₹77.0 L). The gap was funded by a new ₹19.5 L loan, ₹12.6 L released from inventory and recovered supplier advances — cash only moved ₹1.9 L → ₹4.2 L (~3 days of costs).

Action: run a collections drive now; set a receivables ceiling in days of sales (e.g. 15 days) before extending more credit.

Watch

One ledger is 40% of all receivables

The Siliconvalley outlet ledger hit ₹23.2 L by 30 Apr — up from ₹9.2 L at year-end. The Lulu group adds ₹16.3 L net. Two counterparties again hold about two-thirds of a much bigger book.

Action: if the outlet is group-owned, settle inter-unit balances monthly; set credit limits and weekly collection cadence for Lulu.

Improved

Working capital efficiency is sharply better

Inventory fell from 74 days at year-end to ~26 days in May (₹80.8 L → ₹68.2 L while sales doubled). Supplier advances were recovered (₹28.3 L → ₹8.4 L, Falcon ₹17.8 L → ₹2.5 L). The cash conversion cycle is down from ~94 to ~45 days.

Action: the remaining lever is supplier credit — payables still clear in ~2 days; even 15 days of credit frees ~₹40 L at May's purchase rate.

Note

The company runs on director money — and debt is rising

₹1.58 Cr of directors' loans against ₹1 L share capital; reserves still negative (−₹4.3 L). Borrowings rose to ₹86.4 L in May with the new ₹19.5 L Dwideep loan, even as the OD was paid down to ₹20.7 L. Fixed assets grew ₹12.3 L in two months (mainly vehicles).

Action: match new debt to cash-generating assets, and consider converting part of the director loans to equity to strengthen net worth.

Note

The monthly books don't fully tie

Each month's opening reserves differ from the prior closing (by ₹0.9 L in April, ₹2.3 L in May), and April's opening stock (₹81.3 L) differs from the year-end inventory (₹80.8 L). Small, but it means monthly PAT isn't fully reconciled.

Action: ask the accountant to reconcile opening balances before relying on monthly profit figures.
