Insights & recommended actions

What the numbers say, and what to decide next.
Act now

May's margin squeeze came from COGS, not overheads

COGS climbed to 76.5% of revenue in May from 72.0% in April. That 4.6-point shift alone cost ≈ ₹5.0 L of gross profit — the bulk of the ₹5.8 L fall in monthly PAT.

Action: audit May's input costs, pricing and product mix. Simply restoring April's COGS ratio adds back ~₹5 L of profit per month.

Review

Marketing grew 11× faster than revenue

Marketing spend rose 68% MoM (₹3.20 L → ₹5.38 L) while revenue rose 6.2%. In FY 2025-26, marketing at 6.8% of revenue was a major driver of the loss.

Action: attach campaign-level ROI before committing June's budget; hold marketing near 3–4% of revenue until gross margin recovers.

Watch

The break-even cushion nearly vanished in May

At May's 23.5% gross margin, ~₹99 L of monthly revenue is needed to cover the ₹23.2 L of non-COGS costs — May's ₹1.08 Cr cleared it by a whisker. At April's 28.0% margin, break-even was only ~₹74 L.

Action: set a floor gross margin (e.g. 26%+) as the operating target; below it, profit disappears at current overhead levels.

On track

Scale-up has fixed the fixed-cost problem

Revenue run-rate is ~₹1.05 Cr/month vs ₹44.8 L/month averaged across FY 2025-26 (2.3×). Employee cost is down from 10.1% of revenue last year to 8.5% now — overheads are being absorbed.

Action: protect the volume gains; they are what turned last year's loss into this year's profit.

On track

Overheads are well behaved

Employee benefits (+7.1%) and transportation (+7.9%) tracked revenue growth. Finance cost fell 16.3% and other expenses fell 7.5% — no cost-control issue outside COGS and marketing.

Note

Monthly profits are before depreciation & tax

April/May statements book no depreciation; FY 2025-26 carried ₹5.69 L (~₹47 K/month). True monthly PAT is therefore slightly lower than shown — May's thin ₹2.18 L cushion is thinner still.

Action: accrue depreciation monthly so month-end profit reflects reality.
